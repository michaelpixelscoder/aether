# Stories

Stories capture factual observations and experiences.

They are the raw material for understanding real behavior: something that worked well, a recurring frustration, a workaround, an unexpected outcome.
It can be a story about our projects, but also a story about other games or anything external that can serve as a reflexion support (find idea, inspire etc). 

Stories are not solution proposals. Interpretation is useful, but it must remain clearly separated from what was directly seen, heard, or experienced.

## Structure

Give every story its own directory so it can include photos, screenshots, interview notes, or other evidence:

```text
stories/
└── story-name/
    ├── story-name.story.md
    └── supporting-files...
```

Copy `_template/` when recording a story, then rename both the directory and main file. Use a short lowercase kebab-case name. Put the observation date in the document rather than the filename so the story retains a stable path.

## Writing a useful story

- Describe the context: where, when, and who was involved
- Record what happened in concrete terms
- Preserve exact quotes only when permission and accuracy allow it
- State the source and confidence level
- Remove unnecessary personal or sensitive information
- Separate observation, interpretation, and unanswered questions
- Link any exploration inspired by the story from `thoughts/`

Prefer a small, specific account over a broad claim. One observation is evidence that something happened, not proof that it happens everywhere.
